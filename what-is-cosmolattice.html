What is CosmoLattice?

A primer for cosmologists who haven't used it — written for the final project on gauge fields and the GW spectrum from preheating.

This page assumes you know cosmology and field theory but have never opened CosmoLattice. It doesn't assume you know what a lattice simulation is, or why you'd need one. Why a lattice, and why classical fields

Preheating — the violent stage right after inflation, when the inflaton dumps its energy into whatever it's coupled to — is a genuinely non-linear, non-perturbative process. Fields get excited with huge occupation numbers \(n_k \gg 1\) through parametric resonance, at which point they behave classically (a state with $n_k \gg 1$ quanta per mode is, to excellent approximation, a classical field configuration) and standard perturbative QFT — an expansion in small numbers of quanta — stops being the right tool. What replaces it is solving the classical, non-linear field equations directly, numerically, on a spatial grid. That's what a "lattice code" is: not a quantum lattice-QCD calculation, but a classical field theory solved on a discretized box that expands with the universe.

CosmoLattice (Figueroa, Florio, Torrentí & Valkenburg, and — in its 2.0 incarnation — Baeza-Ballesteros, Figueroa, Florio, Loayza, Sattler, Torrentí & Urio) is a modern, public code for exactly this: evolving interacting scalar and gauge fields on a lattice in an expanding FLRW universe. Earlier public codes in this space (LatticeEasy, Defrost, HLattice, PyCOOL, GABE, ...) handle scalar fields only. What CosmoLattice adds — and what makes it the right tool for this project — is gauge fields, U(1) and SU(2), simulated in a way that is exactly gauge invariant on the lattice, not just approximately so in some continuum limit. (User Manual, §2.1–2.2)

The physical setup: three kinds of scalar, two kinds of gauge field

CosmoLattice's field content is fixed once and for all: a real singlet scalar \(\varphi\), a U(1)-charged complex scalar \(\phi\), and an \([SU(N)\times U(1)]\)-charged scalar doublet \(\Phi\) (as many copies of each as you like), together with the Abelian gauge field \(A_\mu\) they can couple to and the non-Abelian gauge field \(B_\mu = B_\mu^a T_a\) the doublet can couple to (currently \(N=2\), so \(T_a=\sigma_a/2\), the Pauli matrices). (Art I, eq. 9; User Manual, eq. 1)

\[ \varphi \in \mathbb{R}\,,\qquad \phi \equiv \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(\phi_0+i\phi_1)\,,\qquad \Phi \equiv \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix}\phi_0+i\phi_1\\ \phi_2+i\phi_3\end{pmatrix}. \]

Interactions between them are set by a single potential \(V(\varphi,|\phi|,|\Phi|)\) — whatever function of the field amplitudes you write down — and gauge invariance is enforced through covariant derivatives and field-strength tensors:

\[ D_\mu^A \equiv \partial_\mu - i g_A Q_A A_\mu\,,\qquad D_\mu \equiv \mathbb{I}D_\mu^A - i g_B Q_B B_\mu^a T_a\,, \] \[ F_{\mu\nu} \equiv \partial_\mu A_\nu - \partial_\nu A_\mu\,,\qquad G_{\mu\nu} \equiv \partial_\mu B_\nu - \partial_\nu B_\mu - i[B_\mu,B_\nu]\,, \]

(Art I, eqs. 10–13)

with \(g_A,\,g_B\) the Abelian and non-Abelian gauge couplings and \(Q_A,\,Q_B\) the charges of whichever field is being covariantly differentiated. The full gauge-invariant action is

\[ S = -\!\int\! d^4x\Big[\tfrac12\partial_\mu\varphi\,\partial^\mu\varphi + (D_\mu^A\phi)^*(D^{A\mu}\phi) + (D_\mu\Phi)^\dagger(D^\mu\Phi) + \tfrac14 F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu} + \tfrac12\mathrm{Tr}\{G_{\mu\nu}G^{\mu\nu}\} + V\Big]\,. \]

(Art I, eq. 8)

This single action already contains every model this project touches: set \(g_A=g_B=0\) and you have a free scalar theory; keep only \(\phi\) and \(A_\mu\) and you have scalar electrodynamics (a U(1)-gauged scalar); keep \(\Phi\) and both gauge fields and you have an SU(2)×U(1) sector, structurally the same field content as the electroweak sector of the Standard Model. The physics question this project asks — how much of the GW spectrum a gauge field adds — is really the question of what changes as you turn \(g_A\) and \(g_B\) on in this one action.

The master equations CosmoLattice solves

Extremizing the action above gives the equations of motion CosmoLattice actually integrates (written here in cosmic time \(t\), scale factor \(a(t)\), for readability — the code itself works in a general time variable and in dimensionless rescaled "program" fields, so that typical numbers stay of order unity regardless of how small \(\hbar\)-scale the physical amplitudes are):

\[ \ddot\varphi - a^{-2}\nabla^2\varphi + 3\frac{\dot a}{a}\dot\varphi = -V_{,\varphi} \] \[ \ddot\phi - a^{-2}D_A^2\phi + 3\frac{\dot a}{a}\dot\phi = -\frac12 V_{,|\phi|}\frac{\phi}{|\phi|} \qquad \ddot\Phi - a^{-2}D^2\Phi + 3\frac{\dot a}{a}\dot\Phi = -\frac12 V_{,|\Phi|}\frac{\Phi}{|\Phi|} \] \[ \partial_0 F_{0i} - a^{-2}\partial_j F_{ji} + \frac{\dot a}{a}F_{0i} = J_A^i \qquad (D_0)_{ab}(G_{0i})_b - a^{-2}(D_j)_{ab}(G_{ji})_b + \frac{\dot a}{a}(G_{0i})_b = (J_i)_a \]

plus the U(1)/SU(2) Gauss constraints, checked (not imposed) throughout the run: \(\partial_i F_{0i}=a^2 J_A^0\), \((D_i)_{ab}(G_{0i})_b = a^2(J_0)_a\) — (User Manual, eqs. 2–8)

and, when the fields are left to source the expansion themselves rather than evolving on a fixed background, the Friedmann equations close the system:

\[ \frac{\ddot a}{a} = -\frac{1}{6m_p^2}\big[\bar\rho+3\bar p\big]\,, \qquad H^2 \equiv \Big(\frac{\dot a}{a}\Big)^2 = \frac{\bar\rho}{3m_p^2}\,, \]

(User Manual, eqs. 9–10)

with \(\bar\rho,\bar p\) the volume-averaged energy and pressure of whatever mix of scalar and gauge fields is present — kinetic, gradient, electric and magnetic contributions from each species, summed. (Art I, eqs. 49–50; User Manual, eqs. 11–12) One sentence worth dwelling on: CosmoLattice does not solve these continuum equations exactly, and does not exactly enforce the continuum Gauss constraints. It solves a set of finite-difference equations built to approximate them at a controlled order in the lattice spacing, using a symplectic (leapfrog/Verlet-family) time integrator — and it's the next section, not this one, that explains how that discretization is built so that it stays exactly gauge-invariant even though it's only approximately solving the continuum theory.

Putting it on a lattice: link variables and exact gauge invariance

The naive way to discretize a gauge theory — replace \(A_\mu(x)\) with its value at lattice sites and finite-difference the covariant derivative — breaks gauge invariance at the level of the discretization itself, because a lattice derivative isn't exactly the continuum one. Lattice gauge theory (the same idea used in lattice QCD) fixes this by not discretizing the gauge field directly at all. Instead it discretizes the parallel transporter between neighbouring lattice sites — the "link" — which by construction transforms exactly like the continuum field strength under a gauge transformation, at any lattice spacing:

U(1) toolkit

\[ V_\mu \equiv e^{-ig_AQ_A\,\delta x_\mu A_\mu} \] \[ V_{\mu\nu} \equiv V_\mu V_{\nu,+\mu} V^*_{\mu,+\nu} V^*_\nu \ \simeq\ e^{-ig_AQ_A\delta x_\mu\delta x_\nu[F_{\mu\nu}+O(\delta x)]} \] \[ (D_\mu^{\pm}\phi)(l) = \pm\frac{1}{\delta x_\mu}\big(V_{\pm\mu}\phi_{\pm\mu}-\phi\big) \]

SU(N) toolkit

\[ U_\mu \equiv e^{-ig_BQ_B\,\delta x_\mu B_\mu^a T_a} \] \[ U_{\mu\nu} \equiv U_\mu U_{\nu,+\mu} U^\dagger_{\mu,+\nu} U^\dagger_\nu \ \simeq\ e^{-ig_BQ_B\delta x_\mu\delta x_\nu[G_{\mu\nu}^aT_a+O(\delta x)]} \] \[ (D_\mu^{\pm}\Phi)(l) = \pm\frac{1}{\delta x_\mu}\big(U_{\pm\mu}\Phi_{\pm\mu}-\Phi\big) \]

(Art I, eqs. 84–85 — the "U(1) toolkit" and "SU(N) toolkit" boxes, reproduced here essentially verbatim)

The link \(V_\mu\) (or \(U_\mu\)) lives on the edge between two lattice sites, not on a site itself, and is exactly unitary (\(V_\mu^*V_\mu=1\), \(U_\mu^\dagger U_\mu=\mathbb{I}\)) by construction — there's no way for it to drift away from a valid gauge configuration the way a naively-discretized \(A_\mu\) could. The smallest closed loop of links around a lattice plaquette, \(V_{\mu\nu}\) or \(U_{\mu\nu}\), plays the role of the field strength \(F_{\mu\nu}\)/\(G_{\mu\nu}\): it's gauge invariant (transforms trivially for U(1), covariantly for SU(N)) and reduces to the continuum field strength as \(\delta x\to0\). Under a gauge transformation \(\phi\to e^{ig_AQ_A\alpha}\phi\), the links transform exactly so as to keep the covariant derivative covariant on the lattice, \((D_\mu^\pm\phi)\to e^{ig_AQ_A\alpha}(D_\mu^\pm\phi)\) — an exact statement, not one that only holds up to lattice-spacing corrections. (Art I, eq. 84, "Gauge Trans." row)

Two practical consequences follow directly from this, and both are why gauge invariance is something this project can actually check rather than just assume:

  • Energy conservation and the Gauss-law constraints (\(\partial_iF_{0i}=a^2J_A^0\), and its SU(2) analogue) are preserved by construction to the accuracy of the time integrator — often down to machine precision for the highest-order integrators — because the spatial discretization is exactly gauge invariant, not merely accurate. (User Manual, §2.3, discussion after eq. 8)
  • CosmoLattice actually runs two flavours of this scheme: a compact formulation that evolves the link variables \(V_\mu\) themselves (exactly gauge invariant, mandatory for SU(N)), and, only in the U(1) case, a non-compact formulation that evolves \(A_\mu\) directly (simpler, but only approximately gauge invariant at finite lattice spacing). Our project's models — lphi4U1 and lphi4SU2U1 — both use the compact, link-variable formulation. (Art I, §5.2–5.3)

Gravitational waves: how the code sources and extracts them

This is the part of CosmoLattice that turns "simulate the fields" into "predict a GW spectrum," and it's where the scalar-vs-gauge question of this project actually lives. Gravitational waves are the transverse-traceless tensor perturbation \(h_{ij}\) on top of the FLRW metric,

\[ ds^2 = -a^{2\alpha}(\eta)\,d\eta^2 + a^2(\eta)(\delta_{ij}+h_{ij})\,dx^idx^j\,, \qquad \partial_ih_{ij}=0\,,\ h_{ii}=0\,, \]

(Art II, eq. 284)

sourced, at linear order, by the transverse-traceless part of the total anisotropic stress of whatever matter is on the lattice:

\[ h_{ij}'' + (3-\alpha)\frac{a'}{a}h_{ij}' - a^{-2(1-\alpha)}\nabla^2h_{ij} = \frac{2}{m_p^2}a^{2\alpha-2}\Pi_{ij}^{TT}\,. \]

(Art II, eq. 285)

This is the equation that makes the project's question concrete: for a theory with our full scalar+gauge field content, the source \(\Pi_{ij}^{\rm eff}\) is

\[ \Pi_{ij}^{\rm eff} = \partial_i\varphi_b\partial_j\varphi_b + 2\,\mathrm{Re}\{(D_i\phi_b)^*(D_j\phi_b)\} + 2\,\mathrm{Re}\{(D_i\Phi_b)^\dagger(D_j\Phi_b)\} - a^{-2\alpha}E_iE_j + a^{-2}B_iB_j - a^{-2\alpha}E_i^cE_j^c + a^{-2}B_i^cB_j^c\,, \]

(Art II, eq. 287)

with \(E_i,B_i\) (\(E_i^c,B_i^c\)) the Abelian (non-Abelian) electric and magnetic fields built from \(F_{\mu\nu}\) (\(G_{\mu\nu}\)). Read literally: every gauge field carries its own, separate source term for GWs — the \(E_iE_j\) and \(B_iB_j\)-type terms — on exactly the same footing as the familiar scalar-gradient term \(\partial_i\varphi\,\partial_j\varphi\). Turning on \(g_A\) or \(g_B\) doesn't just change how the scalar sector evolves and indirectly reshape its gradient-sourced GW signal; it adds a physically distinct source term to \(\Pi_{ij}\) that has no scalar-only counterpart at all. That is the precise sense in which this project's question — how much of the spectrum is "from the gauge field" — is well posed rather than just qualitative.

Two more pieces complete the picture. First, the transverse-traceless projection needed to actually solve for \(h_{ij}\) is non-local (it's a projector in Fourier space, \(\Pi^{TT}_{ij}=\Lambda_{ij,lm}(\hat k)\Pi_{lm}^{\rm eff}\)), which would be prohibitively expensive to apply at every lattice site and time step; CosmoLattice sidesteps this by evolving five unphysical auxiliary fields \(v_{ij}\) (a symmetric traceless tensor) sourced directly by \(\Pi_{ij}^{\rm eff}\) in position space,

\[ v_{ij}'' + (3-\alpha)\frac{a'}{a}v_{ij}' - a^{-2(1-\alpha)}\nabla^2v_{ij} = \frac{2}{m_p^2}a^{2\alpha-2}\Big(\Pi_{ij}^{\rm eff}-\tfrac13\delta_{ij}\Pi_{kk}^{\rm eff}\Big)\,, \]

(Art II, eq. 293)

and only projects to the physical \(h_{ij}=\Lambda_{ij,lm}(\hat k)v_{lm}\) when a measurement is actually needed. Second, the fractional GW energy-density power spectrum — the \(\Omega_{\rm GW}(f)\) this project will report — is defined from the power spectrum of \(h_{ij}'\), normalized to the critical density:

\[ \Omega_{\rm GW}(k,\eta) \equiv \frac{1}{\rho_c}\frac{d\rho_{\rm GW}}{d\log k} = \frac{k^3}{24\pi^2 a^{2\alpha}H^2}\,P_{h'}(k,\eta)\,. \]

(Art II, eq. 301)

The three models this project uses

CosmoLattice ships pre-written models as C++ header files under models/; three of them, sharing the same \(\lambda\varphi^4\)-type inflaton potential, give this project a controlled scalar-vs-gauge comparison without writing a new model from scratch. The base two-field potential (inflaton \(\varphi\) plus one daughter field \(\chi\)) is the packaged lphi4 example:

\[ V(\varphi,\chi) = \frac{1}{p}\lambda\mu^{4-p}|\varphi|^p + \frac12 g^2\varphi^2\chi^2 \ \xrightarrow{p=4}\ \frac{\lambda}{4}\varphi^4 + \frac12 g^2\varphi^2\chi^2\,, \qquad q\equiv\frac{g^2\varphi_*^2}{\omega_*^2}\,, \]

(Art I, eqs. 457, 468, 470 — \(q\) is exactly the q parameter in lphi4.in)

modelfield contentrole in the comparison
lphi4 real singlet inflaton \(\varphi\) + real daughter \(\chi\), potential above, no gauge field. The control. Ordinary scalar self-resonance preheating — this is what "no gauge field" means operationally.
lphi4U1 a single U(1)-charged complex scalar \(\phi\) with \(V=\tfrac{\lambda}{4}|\phi|^4\) (lambda in the input file) and Abelian coupling gU1s \(=g_A\), charge CSU1Charges \(=Q_A\). Same quartic self-coupling, now with the daughter's role played by the gauge field \(A_\mu\) itself — the Abelian electric/magnetic terms in \(\Pi_{ij}^{\rm eff}\) turn on exactly when gU1s does.
lphi4SU2U1 SU(2)-doublet \(\Phi\) as the inflaton (\(V\supset\tfrac{\lambda}{4}|\Phi|^4\)), coupled with strength qG to a real singlet daughter \(\varphi\) and with strength qH to a U(1)-charged complex daughter \(\phi\); gauge couplings gU1s\(=g_A\), gSU2s\(=g_B\). A qualitatively different gauge sector — non-Abelian, two independent couplings \(g_A,g_B\) — rather than just a different coupling strength on the same U(1). Same field-content shape as the electroweak sector.

Potential terms and coupling names above are read directly from CosmoLattice/models/lphi4.h, lphi4U1.h, lphi4SU2U1.h and their default parameter-files/*.in (see the "Reproducing this" section of README.md for the exact build used).

Put together with the GW-sourcing equation above, this is the whole project in one sentence: run the same \(\lambda\varphi^4\)-family potential three times, with the U(1) and SU(2)×U(1) sectors switched on one at a time, and read off how the extra terms in \(\Pi_{ij}^{\rm eff}\) — absent by construction in lphi4 — reshape \(\Omega_{\rm GW}(f)\).
